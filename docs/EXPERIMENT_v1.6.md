# Experiment v1.6 — Strict communication screening

## Principle
No new agent faculties, rewards, semantic labels, or world mechanisms were added. Version 1.6 only makes observer-side screening more conservative and executes a multi-world campaign.

## Strict burst definition
A transient cross-generational communication burst now requires at least three consecutive observer windows in which both environmental encoding and receiver-action uptake exceed shuffled-payload nulls, with encoding excess > 0.10, uptake excess > 0.05, permutation p <= 0.05 for both, >=20 broadcasts and >=20 receiver rows, and >=2 active signalling generations.

## 12-world screen
Three information-fragmentation regimes (0.35, 1.0, 1.65), four seeds each, signal cost 0.03, 600 steps. The previous permissive detector labelled 12/12 runs; the strict detector retains 3/12.

Candidates:
- q=0.35 seed=0: t=400–600, 3 windows, up to 3 active signalling generations.
- q=0.35 seed=1: t=400–600, 3 windows, up to 2 generations.
- q=1.65 seed=1: t=400–600, 3 windows, up to 2 generations.

## Matched readable-vs-censored causal test
At 600 steps and identical signalling costs:
- q=0.35 seed=0: delta harvest +3.25, delta population -2.
- q=0.35 seed=1: delta harvest -114.80, delta population -3.
- q=1.65 seed=1: delta harvest -29.66, delta population +1.

None supports a claim of adaptive language. The current evidence is instead consistent with transient cross-generational informational conventions that can emerge without becoming selectively beneficial.

## Scientific interpretation
The important object is now the birth, persistence, and extinction of proto-conventions. A future stable-language claim requires replication plus positive matched causal fitness effect, persistent receiver uptake, cross-generational continuity, and robustness to null/permutation controls.
