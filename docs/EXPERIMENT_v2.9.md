# v2.9 — Carrier-agnostic falsification and temporal directionality

## Question
Can an already-existing non-genetic carrier show inheritance that cannot be
explained by genetic lineage, persistent internal state, or shared environment?
No new capability, reward, imitation rule, cultural module, or world law is added.

## Existing carriers audited
1. transient anonymous broadcasts;
2. persistent anonymous environmental inscriptions.

## New observer-side controls
- genetic-root × generation conditioned categorical inheritance;
- cluster-free continuous payload dependence after lineage residualization;
- temporal-direction assay: the received payload must predict the receiver's
  later emission more strongly than its prior emission;
- persistent-mark read -> later-inscription inheritance;
- differential cross-lineage exposure of mark variants.

## Findings
Discrete broadcast candidates from v2.7 remain falsified by the genetic-conditioned
null. Persistent marks also fail autonomous inheritance despite high activity.
Continuous payload assays initially show large residual dependencies in multiple
worlds, including late generations. However, these dependencies generally fail the
temporal-direction test: received content does not predict later output better than
prior output. Scrambled evolution preserves much of the mark dependence, confirming
that persistent-state/environmental autocorrelation is a major confound.

A spatial-density probe (30 -> 24 -> 18 world units, unchanged agent rules) increases
encounters and sometimes genealogical depth to G3, but does not yield a candidate
that passes both genetic-conditioning and temporal-direction criteria. Thus lack of
horizontal autonomy is not explained simply by insufficient encounter density.

## Current standard
A candidate second inheritance system must now survive, at minimum:
1. genetic-lineage conditioning;
2. temporal directionality (reception -> later emission > prior emission);
3. real > scrambled content control;
4. multi-lineage / multi-generation participation;
5. variation and differential propagation;
6. faster-than-genetic transmission.

The North Star remains: if a genuinely autonomous faster inheritance system emerges,
ask whether it begins to accumulate predictive knowledge about hidden world laws and
ultimately supports spontaneous scientific discovery without epistemic reward.
